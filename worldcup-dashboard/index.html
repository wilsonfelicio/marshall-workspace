    • Spain win their second World Cup, 16 years after the first. The tournament's top seed closed it out the way they played all summer — control, patience, and the tightest defence in the field, conceding just twice across the entire knockout stage. De la Fuente's side finished on a 38-match unbeaten run; the Rodri–Pedri–Yamal spine never lost its grip. The market had them 59% to lift, our sim 65% — chalk delivered.
    • Ferran Torres, the unlikely final hero. Goalless and tense through 90, the final broke Spain's way in the 106th minute — Torres drilling past Emiliano Martínez in the first minute of extra time. Not Yamal, not Oyarzabal, but Torres with the goal that decides a World Cup.
    • Enzo Fernández's red card broke Argentina. Level and pushing for a winner, Argentina lost their man in stoppage time of regulation — Enzo shown a second yellow for a foul on Cubarsí — and had to face all 30 minutes of extra time with ten. Torres struck almost immediately. Enzo became the sixth player ever sent off in a World Cup final.
    • Messi's last dance ends in silver. At 38, in what he signalled was his final World Cup, Messi dragged the holders to back-to-back finals but couldn't force a second miracle. He finishes on 8 goals and 4 assists, a losing runner-up — and Mbappé (10) keeps the Golden Boot. Argentina's bid for the first repeat since Brazil 1962 falls at the last step.
    • Brazil's summer, in the rear-view. Eliminated in the last 16 by Norway — the earliest exit since 1990 — Brazil watched the old rival lose the final to Europe's best. For Ancelotti it's a rebuild toward 2030; for the neutral Brazilian, no consolation south of the equator this cycle.
