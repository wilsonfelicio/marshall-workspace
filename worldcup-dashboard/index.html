    📰 Storylines — Spain vs Argentina: Europe's Champions Against the World's

    • Spain are 90 minutes from a first men's World Cup since 2010. The tournament's top seed handled France 2–0 in Dallas — Oyarzabal's early penalty, Porro's second-half strike — smothering Mbappé and turning the market's pre-tournament outright favourite out of the draw. De la Fuente's side ride a 37-game unbeaten run and have conceded sparingly all summer; the Rodri–Pedri–Yamal spine has been the most controlled unit in the field. The market makes them ~58% to lift it.
    • Argentina keep winning ugly — and it keeps working. Down 1–0 to England with five minutes left, they struck twice: Enzo Fernández on 85', Lautaro Martínez in stoppage time, both teed up by Messi. It's the holders' third straight knockout decided late (extra time in the two before it). Scaloni's side aren't fluent, but they are 90 minutes from back-to-back World Cups — a feat no nation has managed since Brazil in 1962.
    • Messi's last dance, and the Golden Boot with it. At 38, in what he's signalled is his final World Cup, Messi has dragged Argentina to the final and now leads the scoring race on the assist tiebreak (8 goals, 4 assists) after his double-assist against England. Mbappé, level on 8, can only respond in Saturday's third-place game. One Messi goal in the final likely settles the Boot outright.
    • Brazil's summer is long over, and the neutral's card is CONMEBOL. Eliminated in the last 16 by Norway — the earliest exit since 1990 — Brazil have no thread left in this final beyond continental loyalty: Argentina, the old rival, carrying South America's flag against the best European side of the tournament. For Ancelotti it's already a rebuild toward 2030.
    • The bronze final is a heavyweight consolation. France vs England in Miami on Saturday puts two of the pre-tournament favourites in the third-place game — Mbappé chasing the Boot, Kane and Bellingham (both on 6) with one last shot, and two squads processing near-misses. High quality, low stakes; motivation, not talent, decides these.
