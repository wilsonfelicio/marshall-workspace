# Theme 1 — Banxico & Monetary Policy

*Sample chapter for sign-off. Period covered: 18 Dec 2025 – 26 Jun 2026. Drawn from 52 monetary-policy notes (38 JPM, 14 Citi). Every claim is sourced inline as **[House, DocID, date]**; page numbers are added on **direct quotes and pivotal figures** only — DocIDs map to original files in `00_Chronological_Index.md`. Terminology note: in the JPM notes the analyst writes **"COPOM" to mean Banxico's Junta de Gobierno (Board)**, not Brazil — all such references are Banxico.*

---

## The arc in brief

Over these six months Banxico did three things in sequence: **paused** (after a December cut to 7.00%), delivered **two more 25bp cuts** (March to 6.75%, May to 6.50%), and then **formally closed the easing cycle** and went on hold. The rate path itself was almost boring — 7.00% → 6.75% → 6.50%, ending exactly where both desks had pencilled in back in December. What was *not* boring was the road there: a genuine **dovish detour** in February (when JPM briefly called a 6.25% terminal via three back-to-back cuts), a **hawkish reversal** after a split March decision, and a **late, externally-triggered convergence** in late April when the Governor pre-announced the final cut. By late June the rate sat at 6.50%, both houses saw it parked there, and the live debate had inverted — the *market* was pricing a year-end **hike** that neither desk believed in.

---

## Phase 0 — Starting point: the December cut and the signalled pause (18–23 Dec 2025)

Banxico cut 25bp to **7.00%** at its final 2025 meeting on 18 December — the lowest policy rate since June 2022 — on a **non-consensus 4-1 vote**, and signalled a pause: *"Looking ahead, the Board will evaluate the timing for additional reference rate adjustments"* [Citi C023, 18 Dec 2025, p.2]. At that point the real ex-ante rate was **3.1%** and the Banxico–Fed differential **325bp** [JPM J001, 19 Dec 2025, p.1].

The two desks framed the pause identically but set up subtly different 2026 paths:

- **Citi** read the pause as Banxico buying time to assess the inflation impact of January's tariff and excise-tax adjustments (estimated at 11–25bp from tariffs, ~20bp from excise taxes). It **raised its end-2026 rate call to 6.50% (from 6.25%)**, penciling cuts resuming in **May** and **June** [Citi C023, 18 Dec 2025, p.1–2].
- **JPM** was more hawkish on the number of cuts, expecting *"only one more cut to 6.75%"*, citing recent inflation surprises and January tariffs on non-FTA countries [JPM J001, 19 Dec 2025, p.1]. It already flagged Banxico's *longer*-run intent to take the rate toward the low end of neutral (~2% real, "or 6% nominal") if the economy kept faltering.

So both ended the year expecting roughly 6.50% — but via different routes, with JPM initially seeing fewer cuts.

---

## Phase 1 — Wait-and-see, then a dovish pivot (Jan – 5 Feb 2026)

The December **minutes** (8 Jan) confirmed a pause: most members cited economic slack, a strong peso and anchored expectations, but wanted to see January's tax-hike pass-through first; the lone hawk called Banxico's own inflation path *"non-credible"* [JPM J005, 8 Jan 2026, p.1]. JPM's working call here was a **next cut in June**, with inflation peaking ~4.3% in 1Q and a 3.9% year-end [JPM J005, 8 Jan 2026, p.1].

Then the data turned benign and the tone shifted dovish fast:

- The **2026 Monetary Program** (published ~27 Jan) reaffirmed wait-and-see but framed the tax/tariff effects as limited and transitory, with the Board "not done yet" [JPM J012, 28 Jan 2026, p.1].
- **JPM pivoted hard.** On 28 Jan it flagged a *"meaningful risk of resuming cuts as soon as March"* and even put **25% odds on a 6.00% terminal this year, 10% on 5.50% by mid-2027** [JPM J012, 28 Jan 2026, p.2], helped by 4.5% YTD peso appreciation. A week later (5 Feb) it made it the base case: **three back-to-back 25bp cuts in March, May and June to a 6.25% terminal** — its most aggressive call of the period and *below* Citi [JPM J016, 5 Feb 2026, p.1].
- **Citi** moved in the same direction but less far. Its 30 Jan note still had May/June cuts to 6.50% [Citi C020, 30 Jan 2026, p.3]; by 11 Feb client meetings showed investors seeing *">50bps of cuts this year"* and Citi carried a *"bias toward front-loading to March"* without yet changing the base case [Citi C014, 11 Feb 2026, p.3].

**The 5 February decision: unanimous hold at 7.00%**, but with the door reopened to a cut "as soon as" the next meeting. Banxico staff saw inflation peaking ~4% in 1Q, easing to 3.5% by year-end, converging to target by 2Q27 [JPM J016, 5 Feb 2026, p.1].

> **Divergence at this point (early Feb):** JPM = three cuts to **6.25%**; Citi = two cuts to **6.50%**. JPM was the dove. This gap drove the next two months.

---

## Phase 2 — The March cut and the terminal-rate reversal (13 Feb – 9 Apr 2026)

The **February minutes** (19 Feb) hardened the "March is live" view: the pause *"could be shorter than originally expected"* (a line JPM attributed to Governor Victoria Rodríguez), with only the hawkish deputy favouring a longer hold [JPM J021, 19 Feb 2026, p.1]. **Citi formally changed its call** the same day, front-loading: *"We recently changed our monetary policy call by front-loading the rate cuts to March… and another one in May, reaching a level of 6.50%"* [Citi C007, 19 Feb 2026, p.1–2], and pulled its USDMXN forecast to 18.00 (from 18.87). The 26 Feb **Quarterly Inflation Report** added a dovish growth tilt: Banxico raised its 2026 GDP forecast to **1.6% (from 1.1%)** [Citi C017, 27 Feb 2026, p.5].

But through March a **geopolitical oil shock** (Middle East tensions) complicated the picture and began to erode JPM's aggressive call:

- 13 Mar: JPM still expected a 26 Mar cut but warned *"the risk of a renewed pause in May is gaining traction"* and raised its year-end inflation call to 4.0% [JPM J027, 13 Mar 2026, p.1].
- 20 Mar: JPM sharpened the hedge — *"what is at risk is our call for a terminal rate of 6.25%, not next week's decision"* [JPM J031, 20 Mar 2026, p.1] — and pushed year-end inflation to **4.3%** on a ~US$90/bbl Mexican oil mix.
- Citi, by contrast, held its 6.50% terminal throughout and treated 26 March as a *"close call"* it expected to resolve in a cut [Citi C003, 18 Mar 2026, p.1].

**The 26 March decision: cut 25bp to 6.75% on a 3-2 split** (deputies Borja and Heath dissenting) [JPM J034, 26 Mar 2026, p.1]. Both desks read the split + new, more conditional guidance (*"…the Board will evaluate the appropriateness and timing for an additional reference rate cut"*) as more **data-dependent / hawkish-leaning** than the cut itself [Citi C012, 27 Mar 2026, p.1–2]. The reactions:

- **JPM capitulated on its dovish call**: it abandoned 6.25% and moved to *"a final cut to 6.50% in June after a pause in May"* — explicitly converging up to Citi's long-held 6.50% [JPM J034, 26 Mar 2026, p.1]. It also began calling this the *"longest easing cycle on record."*
- **Citi pushed its final cut out**: *"keeping our end-of-year policy rate forecast of 6.50% but delaying the last 25-bps cut… to at least June (previously May)"* [Citi C012, 27 Mar 2026, p.1–2].

By the **March minutes** (9 Apr, "The Ides of March"), JPM read a neutral message consistent with a **May pause, June final cut**; Citi's read was nearly identical (*"a cut needs more time"*), reiterating "at least June" [JPM J041 / Citi C002, 9 Apr 2026]. The two desks had now **converged** — both at 6.50% terminal via a June cut. Note the irony: the convergence happened only because JPM gave up the dovish call it had held alone.

> Worth holding onto for the cross-theme work: by late March JPM's growth call was already deteriorating fast (2026 GDP cut from 1.5% → 1.3% on 27 Mar, "second downward revision in two weeks"), which fed directly into the rate debate.

---

## Phase 3 — The final cut: an externally-triggered convergence (28 Apr – 7 May 2026)

This is the cleanest example in the whole corpus of **an official communication moving both desks at once**. On **28 April**, Governor Rodríguez told the Senate she expected the Board to *"consider a final cut to our benchmark rate in our next decision"* and that *"we are near the end of the easing cycle we began in May 2024"* [Citi C015, 30 Apr 2026, p.2]. Within 48 hours:

- **JPM** (30 Apr) moved its final cut **forward from June to 7 May**, expecting a 25bp cut to 6.50% and an end-of-cycle signal — while grumbling the front-loading "seems a bit hasty" [JPM J050, 30 Apr 2026, p.1].
- **Citi** (30 Apr) made the identical change: *"We changed our call to a 25-bps rate cut in the May 7 meeting from a pause, reaching a level of 6.50%"* [Citi C015, 30 Apr 2026, p.1–2].

Both also absorbed a grim **flash 1Q26 GDP of −3.0% saar** around this time (Citi cut 2026 growth to 1.1%, JPM toward 1.0%) — reinforcing the case to finish the cuts.

**The 7 May decision: cut 25bp to 6.50% on another 3-2 split**, with an explicit close: *"the Governing Board estimates that it will be appropriate to maintain the reference rate at its current level… thereby conclude the cycle that began in March 2024"* [Citi C010, 8 May 2026, p.1–2]. JPM judged the statement *"slightly hawkish"* (inflation-risk balance reordered toward core persistence) and set its forward call: *"We expect Banxico to stay on hold for the foreseeable future at 6.5% after the longest easing cycle on record (475bps)"* [JPM J057, 7 May 2026, p.2].

---

## Phase 4 — On hold, and the "neutral narrative" (8 May – 26 Jun 2026)

With the cycle closed, the debate shifted from *when's the next cut* to *how long is the hold, and could the next move be a hike.* Three threads ran through this phase:

**1. The Board entrenched the pause.** The **May minutes** (21 May) had *all* members — including the dissenters — agreeing **6.50% is the terminal rate**, with no signal of a move in either direction; Heath was the most hawkish (core/services stickiness, record-low Fed spread) [JPM J068, 21 May 2026, p.1]. The **27 May QIR** then *cut Banxico's own 2026 growth forecast to 1.1% (from 1.6%)* while leaving the inflation path essentially unchanged (3.5% 4Q, convergence only by 2Q27) [JPM J076, 27 May 2026, p.1]. JPM extended its on-hold horizon explicitly to **end-2027** [JPM J076, 27 May 2026, p.2]. The **25 June decision was a unanimous hold at 6.50%** with guidance reaffirmed; Banxico also introduced a **new, fully-sterilised liquidity tool** (auction purchases of BREMS/government securities), which JPM was careful to flag is *not* QE or yield-curve control [JPM J092, 25 Jun 2026, p.2–3].

**2. Fiscal/ratings noise pressed in (handled fully in the Fiscal chapter, flagged here for the rate context).** S&P moved its BBB outlook to **negative** (12 May) and **Moody's downgraded Mexico to Baa3** (stable) on 21–22 May [Citi C016, 13 May 2026; JPM J065, 22 May 2026, p.1] — JPM had pre-called the Moody's move. This sharpened the "fiscal dominance" risk both desks had been watching since January but did not change the rate calls.

**3. The hike that the market priced and the desks rejected.** By mid-June the market was pricing close to a **full 25bp hike by year-end**. Both houses pushed back:
- **Citi**: *"the bar for a rate hike remains high"* and risk is in fact *"tilted more toward lower rates"* [Citi C021, 18 Jun 2026, p.1–2].
- **JPM**: rates *"on hold for the foreseeable future,"* with any future move dictated chiefly by the Fed / relative monetary conditions [JPM J088, 18 Jun 2026; JPM J092, 25 Jun 2026].

The period closed on a **dovish-leaning data note**: 1H-June headline CPI fell to **3.55% oya** (lowest since late 2025, inside the target range), prompting JPM to trim its year-end inflation call to **4.1% (from 4.3%)** and — on a strong April activity print — to *raise* 2026 growth to **1.2% (from 1.0%)** [JPM J094, 26 Jun 2026, p.1–2]. Citi held year-end inflation at **4.4%**, explicitly to absorb a newly-elevated **El Niño** risk (its own estimate: 29–59bp cumulative) [Citi C022, 25 Jun 2026, p.2–3].

---

## How the two desks differed (synthesis)

Substantively JPM and Citi agreed on the destination (6.50%) and the end-of-cycle timing; the differences were in **conviction, sequencing and risk-skew**:

- **Who was the dove, and when.** Counter to the usual stereotype, **JPM was the more aggressive easer** in Feb–Mar (6.25% via three cuts) while **Citi never left 6.50%.** JPM then converged *up* to Citi after the March split. So the "consensus" that formed by April was really JPM abandoning a lonely dovish call.
- **Reading the statements.** Citi consistently labelled the March and May statements as carrying a *"hawkish bias"* / more data-dependent tone; JPM agreed they were "slightly hawkish" in wording but kept framing the *decisions* as net-dovish relative to a strict inflation-targeting reaction function (inflation was running >4.5% when they cut) [JPM J034, 26 Mar 2026; Citi C012, 27 Mar 2026].
- **Post-cycle risk skew.** After 7 May, **Citi saw the risk skewed to the downside (more cuts)** — weak activity, strong peso, conflict de-escalation [Citi C010, 8 May 2026, p.1–2]. **JPM was more symmetric**, leaning on the **Fed/relative-conditions channel** as the swing factor and giving more weight to sticky core-services inflation [JPM J068, 21 May 2026].
- **Growth in the home stretch.** By late June **JPM's growth view recovered faster** (1.2%, on strong April data) than Citi's, which kept trimming its GDP median to 1.1% even as activity firmed [JPM J094, 26 Jun 2026; Citi C022, 25 Jun 2026].

## Caveats & gaps (from the source set)

- **Neutral-range figures drift slightly across notes** (real neutral cited as 1.8–3.6% vs 1.8–3.4%; 6.50% described as both "high-end" and "midpoint" of the corridor at different dates). These are JPM's renderings of Banxico's published range; the corpus has no single fixed original-source number, so a ~0.2pp wobble in the upper bound is left unresolved rather than papered over.
- **The Banxico–Fed differential** is given as a hard number only early (325bp, 19 Dec 2025); later notes describe it qualitatively as "compressing / record-low" without restating a figure, so no precise end-June spread can be quoted from these documents alone.
- JPM "daily" and "weekly" notes on the same/adjacent dates (e.g. 8 May, 22 May, 29 May, 18 Jun) are **overlapping companion pieces**; treated as corroborating, not independent, data points. No internal contradictions were found.
