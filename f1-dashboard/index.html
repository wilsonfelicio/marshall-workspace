F1's original night race (2008) and still its most brutal: 19 corners, zero rest, bumpy public roads lined with walls, run in ~30°C and 80% humidity under floodlights. Drivers lose 3–4 kg and the race routinely pushes the 2-hour limit. The 2023 removal of the old T16–T19 chicane under the grandstand opened a ~400 m flat-out stretch and made the lap faster — but passing is still hard-earned: T7 (Memorial) and T13 hairpin are the only honest braking-zone moves. Lap record 1:34.486 (Ricciardo, 2024).

The night race. Singapore invented the F1 night race in 2008 and it's still the template: ~1,600 floodlight projectors, walls close enough to touch, and a track so demanding that every edition until 2024 had at least one safety car. The humidity is the real opponent — cockpit temps exceed 55°C and drivers train in sauna suits for weeks beforehand. Historically a Red Bull/Mercedes-resistant track where downforce and driver commitment beat engine power; it's also where title campaigns go to die (ask Vettel '17 or Hamilton '08).
Winners' roll: Alonso ('08, '10), Hamilton ('09, '14, '17, '18), Vettel ('11, '12, '13, '15, '19), Webber–Rosberg–Ricciardo era in between ('14–'16 Mercedes/Red Bull trade), Pérez ('22), Sainz ('23), Norris ('24), Russell ('25). Of the current grid: Hamilton ×4, Alonso ×2, Sainz, Norris and Russell have won here. The famous absentee: Verstappen has never won Singapore — the only current-calendar race missing from his CV.
Where to pass: Two honest moves: the heavy braking into T7 (Memorial corner) at the end of Raffles Boulevard, and the T13 hairpin after the run past Anderson Bridge. T1 works on lap 1 and restarts. Elsewhere it's trains — which is why qualifying is ~70% of the job and an early safety car window decides strategy. Since 2023 the flat-out blast where the old T16–T19 grandstand chicane used to be gives a fourth DRS zone and a half-chance into the final corners.
Iconic Marina Bay Moments
  • 💥 2008 — Crashgate: Nelson Piquet Jr. deliberately crashed at T17 to trigger a safety car that handed teammate Alonso the win. The biggest fixing scandal in F1 history — it surfaced a year later and ended Flavio Briatore's team career. The inaugural night race was rigged.
  • 🦎 2015/2016 — Uninvited guests: A man calmly walked along the live track in 2015 (Vettel, leading, reported "there's a man on the track" mid-race); giant monitor lizards have wandered across in practice more than once. Street racing in the tropics.
  • 🎯 2017 — The title-deciding start crash: Vettel, Räikkönen and Verstappen collided in the spray before Turn 1. Both Ferraris out in seconds, Hamilton won from P5, and the 2017 championship effectively ended right there — on a track where Ferrari had been fastest all weekend.
  • 🏆 2019 — Vettel's last dance: An undercut on teammate Leclerc gave Vettel his 5th Singapore win — the 53rd and final victory of his career. Marina Bay bookended his peak: most wins here of anyone.
  • 🛑 2023 — The streak breaker: Sainz defended with DRS chess (deliberately towing Norris to hold off the Mercedes charge) to end Red Bull's record 15-race win streak — the only race Red Bull lost all season. Singapore stayed Verstappen-proof.
  • 🧊 2024 — No safety car, finally: Norris dominated by 20+ seconds in the first Singapore GP ever run without a safety car — ending a 100% record that had stood since 2008. Russell won under the lights a year later; he's the defending winner this weekend, and he starts from the back.

📰 Key Stories Heading Into Singapore

  • Russell starts from the BACK — His engine let go while running P3 at Sepang, and the replacement is power unit #5 — one over the season allocation. Back-of-grid start at the track where qualifying matters most, as the defending Singapore winner, 84 points down with seven rounds left. He was the form driver of the season (Baku grand slam, three wins); now his title challenge probably dies on Sunday. Brutal doesn't cover it.
  • First-ever Marina Bay sprint — One 60-minute practice session, then straight into Sprint Qualifying on the bumpiest, walliest street track on the calendar. Teams arrive with setups locked from the sim, and a Friday mistake cascades through the whole weekend. Extra sprint points also mean Antonelli can stretch the lead further before Sunday even starts.
  • Antonelli: 84 up, seven to go — 320 points, 8 wins, and mathematically safe from being caught this weekend going wrong: he can't clinch here, but Austin comes into play next. At 20 years old he'd smash Vettel's youngest-champion record by three years. With Russell at the back, his main job Sunday is avoiding the walls.
  • Verstappen's missing trophy — Fresh off his first win of 2026 at Sepang and 0.196s short in Baku, Max arrives at the only current track he's never won. Red Bull's 2026 package has been strongest on power tracks though, and Marina Bay is the opposite — downforce, traction, patience. Tied with Norris at 188 for P5.
  • The P3 scrap — Hamilton 214, Leclerc 191, Norris 188, Verstappen 188: four drivers covered by 26 points behind the Mercedes pair. Ferrari trail Mercedes by 151 in the constructors and need damage limitation to hold P2 from McLaren (405 vs 316) with Red Bull (298) closing.
  • Survival mode — Final leg of the triple-header: Baku → Sepang → Singapore, finishing with the two most physical races of the year back-to-back in the tropics. Two hours at 30°C/80% humidity under the lights, with evening thunderstorms in the forecast all three days. A wet sprint-format Singapore would be genuinely unprecedented chaos.
