Hermann Tilke's first F1 circuit (1999) and still his best-regarded: two ~900 m straights back-to-back, split only by the tight T15 hairpin, so a mistake at the last corner gets punished for the next two kilometres. The rest is a flowing mix of fast esses (T5–T6), long double-apex rights (T7–T8) and generously wide corner entries that offer multiple lines — overtaking here needs no gimmicks. Add 32°C, ~80% humidity and afternoon monsoons, and it's one of the most physical races of the year. Lap record 1:34.080 (Vettel, 2017).

The template track. Sepang was the first purpose-built Tilke circuit and the blueprint for two decades of F1 venues — but unlike most of its descendants it's genuinely loved: huge width on corner entry (multiple viable lines into T1, T4, T9 and T15), fast flowing esses, and the signature double-straight/hairpin combo that makes slipstream chess out of every restart. It hosted the Malaysian GP from 1999 to 2017, then fell off the calendar over hosting fees — the government said as recently as 2025 there were no plans to return. It took a war to bring F1 back: the Bahrain GP, displaced by the Iran conflict, lands here with Bahrain footing the bill and keeping the name.
Winners' roll: Irvine (1999), M. Schumacher (2000, '01, '04), R. Schumacher ('02), Räikkönen ('03, '08), Alonso ('05, '07, '12), Fisichella ('06), Button ('09), Vettel ('10, '11, '13, '15), Hamilton ('14), Ricciardo ('16), Verstappen ('17). Of the current grid only Alonso (×3), Hamilton and Verstappen have won here — and Verstappen was the last man to do it. Bonus trivia: Sepang 2017 was Pierre Gasly's F1 debut.
Where to pass: The main event is the double-straight sequence — exit T14 onto the ~900 m back straight, boost zone into the T15 hairpin, then if you don't make it stick (or you do), the tow continues down the pit straight into T1, which is so wide that around-the-outside works. T4, after the uphill run from T2–T3, is move number three. Tyre management matters: the long fast rights (T7–T8, T12–T13) grind the fronts in the heat, and track position means little if your rubber dies five laps early.
Iconic Sepang Moments
  • 🎁 1999 — The gifted win: Schumacher returned from his broken leg, dominated, and twice waved Irvine past to help his title bid. Both Ferraris were then disqualified over bargeboard dimensions — and reinstated on appeal a week later. Sepang's first race came with a courtroom epilogue.
  • 🌧️ 2001 — Twin spins: A sudden downpour sent both Ferraris spinning at the same corner on lap 3. Schumacher recovered from the gravel and the chaos of a six-car pileup era to win anyway — one of the great wet-weather recovery drives.
  • 🌊 2009 — The monsoon: The race started at 17:00 for European TV; the storm won. Red-flagged on lap 31 in near-darkness with cars aquaplaning off the straight, never restarted — Button won with half points awarded, one of the rarest results in F1 history.
  • 🗡️ 2013 — "Multi 21": The most famous ignored team order ever. Vettel attacked Webber after both were told to hold station, took the win, and the podium room turned arctic. Webber: "Multi 21, Seb." The phrase outlived both careers.
  • 💥 2016 — "Oh no, no!": Hamilton's engine detonated in flames while cruising to victory with 15 laps left — the failure that arguably handed Rosberg the 2016 title. Ricciardo inherited the win; Hamilton sat in the grass watching the smoke.
  • 👶 2017 — The kid wins: One day after his 20th birthday, Verstappen passed Hamilton around the outside and drove off into the distance. Nobody knew it would be Sepang's last F1 race for nine years.

📰 Key Stories Heading Into Sepang

  • F1's back in Malaysia — because of a war — This is the Bahrain GP in name only: displaced from Sakhir by the Iran conflict, relocated to Sepang under a deal where Bahrain covers the costs and keeps the proceeds. It's the first F1 race here since October 2017, meaning zero current-car data for anyone — most of this grid has never raced the track at all. The sim teams earned their salaries this month.
  • Antonelli: 66 up, eight to go — Baku was his messiest weekend in months (crash-compromised qualifying, P16 grid) and he still salvaged P5 to keep the lead at 302–236. He can't clinch this weekend, but every race where Russell fails to take ~20 points out of him effectively ends it. At 20, he'd shatter Vettel's youngest-champion record by three years.
  • Russell is the form driver — A grand slam in Baku: pole by 0.837s, led every lap, fastest lap, and won by 0.196s over Verstappen — the closest finish since the 2002 US GP. Three wins now (Australia, Austria, Baku) and the only man realistically between Antonelli and the title. He needs to start converting form into 25-point swings immediately, and Sepang's heat is a Mercedes-friendly unknown.
  • Verstappen: all podiums, no wins — Five podiums in the last six rounds including P2 in Madrid and Baku, still zero victories in 2026. Baku brought Red Bull's first double podium since China 2024 (Hadjar third, back from injury and now P8 in the championship). Two 900 m straights play to the Red Bull Ford's strengths — and the last time F1 raced here, Verstappen won it.
  • Ferrari need a response — Hamilton (199) and Leclerc (179) hold P3 and P5, but Ferrari's last win was Monza-adjacent history now: Baku was a 6th/4th afternoon while Mercedes stretched the constructors gap to 160 points. Norris (186) sits between the two red cars — P3 through P5 covered by 20 points with eight rounds left.
  • Survival notes — Middle leg of a triple-header (Baku → Sepang → Singapore): two of the most physical races of the year back-to-back in the tropics. Colapinto carries a five-place grid penalty for the Baku pile-up that also collected Gasly and Norris. And the 15:00 start sits right in the daily thunderstorm window — the 2009 monsoon started exactly this way.
