Short, bumpy, anticlockwise — one of only six left-handers on the calendar. Named for F1 driver José Carlos Pace (1975 winner here, killed in a '77 plane crash). Built in 1940 in a bowl between two lakes (inter lagos) with the Senna S plunging downhill into Turn 1, a long uphill blast through Subida dos Boxes onto the pit straight, and medium-speed infield corners that reward chassis balance over outright power. Short lap = mixed strategies = usually the best race of the season. Lap record 1:10.540 (Bottas, 2018). Pirelli brings C2/C3/C4 — one step harder than Mexico.

Built in a bowl between the lakes. Construction began in 1938 on farmland south of São Paulo — the name Interlagos literally means "between lakes" (Guarapiranga and Billings). Blueprints were lifted from Brooklands, Roosevelt Raceway and Montlhéry, and the track opened in 1940 as a banked monster you could run as a giant oval. Shortened to the current infield layout in 1990. Renamed in 1985 for José Carlos Pace, Brazil's 1975 Interlagos winner, killed in a light-plane crash two years later.
Winners' roll (modern era): Hamilton ×3 ('16, '18, '21 sprint-to-win drive from P10), Verstappen ×2 ('19, '24-sprint-and-race in the rain), Norris ('25), Sainz ('23), Russell ('22), Bottas ('21), Pérez ('22 sprint), Vettel ('13, '10), Button ('09 title). Of the current grid, Hamilton, Verstappen, Russell, Sainz, Bottas, Pérez, Norris and Alonso have stood on the top step here. Interlagos has decided more titles than any track still on the calendar outside Abu Dhabi: Hunt '76, Prost '83/'85/'89, Senna '91 (home win), Schumacher '00, Räikkönen '07, Hamilton '08, Button '09, Hamilton '19.
Where to pass: Short lap, big elevation, loads of overtaking. Zone one: Senna S (T1–T2) — a late-brake left-hander plunging downhill, with DRS off the pit straight. Zone two: Descida do Lago (T4) — second DRS, used for second-move slipstreaming. Zone three: braking for Bico de Pato (T10). The 1.2 km uphill run from Juncão (T12) back to the start-finish is the longest flat-out section — chassis balance here decides whether you're a sitting duck or the predator onto the Senna S. Weather is the great equaliser: Interlagos is afternoon-thunderstorm country and two of the last four races here have been rained on.
Iconic Interlagos Moments
  • 🇧🇷 1991 — Senna on seven gears: Ayrton won his home race for the first time with his McLaren stuck in sixth gear for the final laps, body cramping so badly he couldn't lift the trophy on the podium. The image of him collapsed in the car, screaming into the radio, is the single most-replayed Interlagos moment.
  • 💔 2007 — Räikkönen steals it on the last page: Hamilton arrived with a four-point lead, lost electrics early, finished P7. Alonso couldn't keep up. Räikkönen won the race AND the title by one point — the only Finn to do both on the same afternoon.
  • 🏆 2008 — "Is that Glock?": The last corner of the last lap of the season. Hamilton needed P5 and Glock was slipping on dry tyres in the drizzle. Hamilton got past at Junção on the final lap and clinched his first title by ONE point, Massa crying on the podium having won the race. Still the greatest title finish in F1 history.
  • 🌧️ 2016 — Verstappen in the rain: From P16 to P3 in a wet race with a late tyre gamble nobody else dared. The pass around the outside of Rosberg at Juncão and the near-save coming out of the Senna S are still in the F1 highlight reel. Max was 19.
  • 🎭 2019 — Gasly beats Hamilton to the line: Toro Rosso's Pierre Gasly held off a charging Hamilton by 0.062s on the drag to the flag after a safety-car restart. Only his second podium of all time. Verstappen won, Albon spun by Hamilton, and the Mercedes-Honda politics boiled for weeks.
  • ⚡ 2021 — Hamilton's drive of the decade: Disqualified from quali, 20-place engine penalty, started the sprint from P20. Won the sprint. Started the race from P10. Won the race. 25 overtakes across two days against a title-leading Verstappen — the drive even his critics admit.
  • 💧 2024 — Quali chaos, Max from P17: Qualifying ran into Sunday morning after biblical rain. Verstappen started P17, drove through the field in the rain to win, and the title fight was effectively over. The best wet-weather drive of the current era.

📰 Key Stories Heading Into Interlagos

  • Youngest ever champion arrives home-ish — Antonelli clinched in Mexico with P3, becoming F1's youngest ever world champion at 20 years, 2 months, 7 days — three years and change clear of Vettel's 2010 record. His 9th win of the year was Austin. Interlagos is his first race as champ — expect the Mercedes garage to fully let him off the leash now that the maths is done.
  • Mercedes needs just three points — and they can take the double on Sunday — 630 vs Ferrari's 504, 126-point gap, 86 still on the table after Brazil. Outscore Ferrari by 3+ and it's done. With Antonelli flying, Russell solid and Ferrari not having won since Hamilton's summer breakthrough, this is Mercedes's title to lose in Wilson's backyard. Hasn't happened since 2020.
  • Bortoleto's home race — a national event — Gabriel Bortoleto is the first Brazilian on the grid since Massa in 2017. He's sitting on 14 points in a developing Sauber (soon-to-be Audi) that's finally found the back end. Scored in Mexico. The Interlagos grandstands have been waiting for this since Felipe left — expect chant-level noise every time his car crests the hill.
  • Three drivers for P2 overall — Verstappen 261, Hamilton 259, Russell 250, Leclerc 245. Max is the form pick (won in Mexico, Austin P3, already won here twice), Hamilton wants to add Interlagos to his podium wall as a Ferrari driver, Russell is Mercedes's other half of the constructors push. All four are separated by 16 points with four races to go.
  • Red Bull vs McLaren for P3 — nine points — Red Bull 380, McLaren 371. McLaren has crumbled in the autumn (one win since July, Piastri now 77 points behind Norris) while Hadjar + Verstappen are dragging Red Bull back up. Interlagos usually rewards good mechanical grip and tyre management — slight McLaren circuit on paper, but form says otherwise.
  • Weather will decide this one — São Paulo is deep into rainy season and two of the last four races here have been wet. FP2 and quali Saturday afternoon are the most exposed slots on the schedule. If it rains, bet on Verstappen; if it doesn't, bet on Mercedes.
