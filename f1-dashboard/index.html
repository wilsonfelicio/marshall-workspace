The thin-air circuit: at 2,285 m the air is ~25% less dense, so cars run Monaco-spec wings but get Monza-level downforce and grip. Cooling, braking and the hybrid all suffer — this is the race engineers dread. The 1.2 km run to Turn 1 is the longest flat-out blast to a first corner all season, and the Foro Sol stadium section (built inside an old baseball ground) is the loudest corner complex in F1. Lap record 1:17.774 (Bottas, 2021). Pirelli brings its softest trio: C3/C4/C5.

The brothers' track. Built in the Magdalena Mixhuca park in 1959 and named for Ricardo and Pedro Rodríguez — Mexico's racing princes who both died in cockpits. Ricardo was killed here in 1962 practice, aged just 20, at the fearsome banked Peraltada; Pedro died at the Norisring in 1971. F1 has come and gone in three eras (1963–70, 1986–92, 2015–today), and the modern layout cuts through the Foro Sol baseball stadium where the old Peraltada used to run — trading F1's scariest corner for its loudest grandstand.
Winners' roll (modern era): Verstappen ×5 ('17, '18, '21, '22, '23) — the track record. Rosberg ('15), Hamilton ×2 ('16, '19), Sainz ('24, his Ferrari farewell-season win), Norris ('25). Of the current grid, Verstappen, Hamilton, Sainz and Norris have won here. Titles have been settled in Mexico too: Surtees in '64, Hunt's famous run in '76 was at Fuji but Hamilton clinched '17 and '18 right here — both times mid-pack after first-lap contact.
Where to pass: The 1.2 km drag to T1 is the overtaking spot — 360+ km/h in the thin air down to ~100, with slipstream + override making lap-1 three-wide arrivals a tradition. Zone two is the run to T4, zone three the braking for T12 into the stadium. Thin air means long braking distances and weak aero wake punishment — following is easier here than almost anywhere. Watch the T1–T3 run-off: cutting the first complex ("the optional Turn 1") is this year's talking point again.
Iconic Mexico Moments
  • 🏆 1964 — Three-way title finale: Clark, Hill and Surtees all arrived with a shot. Clark's engine died with two laps left, and Lorenzo Bandini waved teammate Surtees through to P2 on the final lap — handing him the championship by one point. Still the closest thing to a scripted ending F1 has produced by accident.
  • 🚷 1970 — The crowd that stopped F1: 200,000 fans broke the fences and sat on the grass verges lining the track. Pedro Rodríguez personally toured the circuit begging them to move. The race ran anyway — a dog was hit, drivers were furious — and F1 refused to return for 16 years.
  • 💪 1990 — Mansell around the outside: The pass of the era — Mansell swept around the outside of Berger through the banked Peraltada at ~290 km/h, a corner where lifting was brave and passing was lunacy. Prost won from P13 that day; the podium was pure Ferrari-Williams vintage.
  • 🎙️ 2016 — The podium that changed three times: Verstappen crossed P3, got a penalty while in the cooldown room; Vettel inherited it, delivered his infamous expletive-laden radio rant at Charlie Whiting — then lost it hours later for a Ricciardo block. Three podium finishers in one evening.
  • 👑 2017 — Title clinched from P9: Vettel and Hamilton collided at Turn 3 on lap 1; Hamilton drove back through the field with a punctured race and clinched title #4 anyway, while Verstappen disappeared up the road. Mexico loved it — the party didn't care who won.
  • 🐂 2021 — Max's triple-slipstream: From P3 on the grid, Verstappen towed past both Mercedes and swept around the outside of Hamilton into Turn 1 — race effectively over in 800 metres. Pérez took his first home podium and the Foro Sol nearly came down.

📰 Key Stories Heading Into Mexico City

  • Match point #2 — and this one's hard to miss — Antonelli leads Russell 365–242 with 125 points left. The only way the title fight survives Mexico: Russell wins AND Antonelli finishes P9 or lower. Anything else and the 20-year-old is champion on Sunday — youngest ever by three clear years (Vettel was 23), in his second season, with four rounds to spare. He arrives having won in Austin, his 9th of the year.
  • Checo's 300th, at home, on Día de Muertos — Pérez becomes the sixth driver to reach 300 GP starts, in front of the Foro Sol, in the weekend the city celebrates its dead. The romance ends there: Cadillac is still on zero points and Pérez is batting away retirement questions. Last year was the first home race without a Mexican driver since 2015 — expect the loudest grandstand of the season regardless of where he qualifies.
  • Four drivers, nine points, one P2 — Russell 242, Hamilton 241, Verstappen 236, Leclerc 233. Best-of-the-rest is the real championship now, and Verstappen is the form pick: five Mexico wins, victories in Bahrain and Singapore this autumn, P3 at Austin.
  • Red Bull vs McLaren: dead level — 355–355 for P3 in the constructors after Red Bull's autumn surge (Verstappen + Hadjar) caught a McLaren that has scored one win since July. Mercedes (607) needs a handful of points to seal the constructors title; Ferrari (474) is safe in P2.
  • Thin air, soft tyres — Pirelli brings the C3/C4/C5 softest trio to the hardest cooling race of the year. 25% less air density means Monza grip with Monaco wings, long braking zones, and easy following — plus the annual argument about drivers cutting the T1–T3 complex ("Mexico's optional Turn 1").
  • Interlagos is NEXT WEEK — Nov 6–8 closes the triple-header. If Russell somehow keeps the title alive on Sunday, it gets decided at your home race.
