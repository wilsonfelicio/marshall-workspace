⚠️ Sprint weekend — the first ever F1 Sprint on Dutch soil, and the last-ever F1 race at Zandvoort. Only one hour of practice before it starts counting: FP1 Friday morning, then straight into Sprint Qualifying that afternoon. Get the setup wrong and there's no time to fix it. Weather this far out isn't locked — but Zandvoort sits right on the North Sea, so treat every forecast as provisional: sea breeze, fast-moving cloud and a genuine rain risk are the norm here (2020–23 all had wet or damp running). Mild, ~18–21°C. On a narrow, banked, track-position circuit, one damp session can flip the whole order — and with two qualifying sessions (Sprint + GP), Friday and Saturday both matter.

A 1948 dune-side classic in the seaside town of Zandvoort, reborn for F1 in 2021 after 36 years away. It's the only track on the calendar with banked corners — the Hugenholtzbocht (Turn 3) and the flat-out final Arie Luyendyk bend (Turn 14) are banked at ~18°, letting cars carry huge speed onto the main straight. The lap is a fast, flowing, roller-coaster ribbon threaded through the dunes with almost no run-off and barely a moment to breathe. Overtaking is genuinely hard: the heavy braking into the Tarzan hairpin (Turn 1) off the DRS main straight is the one real passing spot, so grid position and clean qualifying laps are gold. Coastal wind and shifting grip make it a driver's track.

Most wins (driver): Jim Clark holds the all-time record with 4 Dutch GP victories in the 1960s. In the modern era Max Verstappen is king of his home race, winning three in a row (2021–2023) before McLaren broke the streak.
Most constructor wins: Ferrari lead the all-time Zandvoort count with 8 wins, stretching back to Alberto Ascari's win in the very first World Championship Dutch GP in 1952.
Lap record: 1:11.097 — Lewis Hamilton (Mercedes, 2021). The outright fastest lap ever seen here is Oscar Piastri's 1:08.662 in 2025 qualifying, on the reprofiled, banked modern layout.
The real overtake spot: the heavy braking into the banked Tarzan hairpin (Turn 1) at the end of the DRS main straight is the one genuine passing place. The banked final corner (Arie Luyendyk) is taken almost flat, firing cars onto that straight — carry speed through it and you get a run. Everywhere else the narrow dune ribbon makes passing brutal: it's a qualifying and track-position circuit, and this weekend there are two grids to nail (Sprint + GP).
Iconic Moments
  • 🏁 1952 — the beginning: Alberto Ascari won the first World Championship Dutch GP at Zandvoort for Ferrari, on his way to the title. The dune circuit became an instant classic.
  • 🕊️ 1973 — tragedy and change: Roger Williamson's fatal accident shocked the sport and became a landmark moment in the push for better marshalling and trackside safety.
  • ⚔️ 1985 — Lauda's last win: In his final season, Niki Lauda held off McLaren teammate Alain Prost by barely two tenths for the 25th and last victory of his career — then Zandvoort fell off the calendar for 36 years.
  • 🧡 2021 — the orange homecoming: F1 returned after 36 years and Max Verstappen won in front of a sea of orange, taking the championship lead from Hamilton in a title year that ended in Abu Dhabi drama.
  • 👑 2021–2023 — Verstappen's hat-trick: Three straight home wins from pole, each in front of 300,000+ fans. For three years, Zandvoort belonged to Max.
  • 🧡 2024–2025 — papaya takes over: Lando Norris ended the streak with a dominant 2024 win, then Oscar Piastri won in 2025 and set the fastest lap ever seen at the track — back-to-back home defeats for Verstappen heading into the farewell.

📰 Key Stories Heading Into the Netherlands

  • Farewell, Zandvoort — the last dance in the dunes — This is the final F1 race ever at Circuit Zandvoort, closing a six-year chapter that reopened in 2021. When the flag falls Sunday, F1 leaves the Dutch coast indefinitely. Expect a full-throated orange send-off — and every driver wanting their name on the last-ever winners' list.
  • First Sprint on Dutch soil — chaos multiplier — Zandvoort has never run the Sprint format before, and it lands with only one hour of practice before things count. A narrow, banked, low-run-off track plus a compressed schedule and 8 bonus points on Saturday is a recipe for a shaken-up order. Two qualifying sessions mean Friday afternoon matters almost as much as Saturday.
  • Antonelli heads in as runaway leader — The rookie leaves the summer break 50 clear of Hamilton and 59 up on teammate Russell after a P3 in Hungary. Zandvoort's precision-and-banking demands suit a planted car, and the Mercedes has been the class of the field. A clean weekend here and the title is increasingly his to lose.
  • Norris arrives with momentum — and history here — Lando took his first win of 2026 at the Hungaroring and comes to a track he dominated in 2024. McLaren's upgraded car finally looks like a race-winner again. He's P5 and 91 back, but if the papaya form is real, Zandvoort is where he starts eating into the Mercedes lead.
  • Verstappen's last shot at home — and he hasn't won here since 2023 — Max was beaten at Zandvoort by Norris (2024) and Piastri (2025), and now gets one final chance in front of the orange army, coming off a strong P2 in Hungary. Red Bull's form in banked, high-load corners is the question mark. If he delivers, it's the send-off script the whole grandstand is praying for.
  • Mercedes intra-team tension simmers — Russell (P3) needs to stop the bleeding to teammate Antonelli, now 59 points up the road. Zandvoort's flowing layout and banking have historically suited the Silver Arrows, so both should be near the front — which is exactly when the team-order questions get awkward.
  • Ferrari's downforce test — and Hamilton still chasing that first red win — Hamilton (P2) and Leclerc (P4) keep the Scuderia best-of-the-rest behind Mercedes. The high-downforce, banked Zandvoort could flatter the red cars, but they've been out-qualified all year. Lewis owns the lap record here (2021) and is still hunting a maiden victory in Ferrari colours.
  • Track position is everything — twice — Overtaking at Zandvoort is limited to the braking zone into Tarzan; the rest is a qualifying track. With a Sprint grid and a race grid to set, whoever masters the banked, low-grip ribbon on Friday and Saturday controls the weekend. Saturdays win championships here.
