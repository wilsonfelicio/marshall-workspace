The whole weekend is shifted a day: Thursday–Saturday format, so the race is Saturday morning 08:00 BRT — coffee-and-couch timing. Baku in late September is mild (~25°C) but the Caspian wind is the wildcard: gusts change braking points lap to lap and have caught out champions here. Walls everywhere, the highest safety-car hit rate of any current street track, and huge slipstream effects — qualifying position matters less here than almost anywhere.

A circuit of extremes: the second-longest lap on the calendar combines a Monaco-tight old town with a ~2.2 km flat-out blast along the Caspian seafront — the longest full-throttle stretch in F1. The signature is the castle section (T8–T12): 7.6 m wide at its narrowest, uphill, squeezed between the walls of the 12th-century Icherisheher old city. Get it wrong there and the wall collects you; get the last corner right and the slipstream down the main straight gives you a run into T1 no defender can fully cover. The track sits ~28 m below sea level — the lowest point F1 visits all year. Lap record 1:43.009 (Leclerc, 2019).

Chaos is the brand. No circuit on the calendar has a wilder box score: nine races, eight different winners, multiple leaders crashing out or losing wins in the final laps. Until 2025 no polesitter had ever converted pole into victory here — the slipstream down the 2.2 km blast is so strong that leading onto the main straight is often a disadvantage. Safety cars, red flags and last-lap plot twists are the norm, not the exception.
Winners' roll: Rosberg (2016), Ricciardo (2017, from P10), Hamilton (2018), Bottas (2019), Pérez (2021, 2023), Verstappen (2022, 2025), Piastri (2024). Pérez earned the "King of Baku" tag; Verstappen's 2025 pole-to-flag run was the first in the circuit's history. Of the current grid, Hamilton, Verstappen, Pérez and Piastri are the past winners on site.
Where to pass: T1 after the monster straight is the prime zone — braking from ~360 km/h in the tow down to ~100. T3, at the end of the second boost zone, is move number two. The downhill T15 catches lungers, and the entire final sector is about maximizing exit from T16 to carry the slipstream. Track position is cheap here; tyre life and timing your safety cars are everything.
Iconic Baku Moments
  • 😡 2017 — Road rage under the safety car: Vettel drove into the side of Hamilton's Mercedes in a fit of pique, Hamilton's headrest came loose, and Ricciardo won from P10 with a triple overtake into T1. An 18-year-old Lance Stroll took his first podium, pipped to P2 by Bottas at the line.
  • 💥 2018 — Red Bull civil war: Ricciardo and Verstappen crashed into each other on the main straight after 40 laps of wheel-banging; Bottas then lost the win to a debris puncture with three laps left. Hamilton inherited it.
  • 🎈 2021 — Blowout and "brake magic": Verstappen's tyre exploded at ~320 km/h while leading with five laps left; at the standing restart, Hamilton knocked his brake-balance switch and sailed straight on from P2. Pérez won a race neither of the title rivals scored in.
  • 🛡️ 2024 — Piastri's masterclass: Piastri passed Leclerc into T1 with a lap-20 lunge and then defended for 30 laps under constant attack — while Pérez and Sainz took each other out on the penultimate lap fighting for the podium.
  • 🏰 The castle section: 7.6 m wide between medieval walls — Leclerc's "I am stupid" radio (2019 quali crash) is one of many bills paid there. Charles has three poles here and zero wins, the Baku curse in one stat.
  • 🚀 2016 — 366 km/h: Bottas' Williams hit 366 km/h in the race with a tow — among the highest speeds ever recorded in an F1 race, and the reason engineers still argue about wing levels all weekend here.

📰 Key Stories Heading Into Baku

  • Antonelli, 81 clear with nine to go — Won Madrid from P2 for victory number eight of the season and now leads Russell 292–211. He can't clinch in Baku (233 points remain after this weekend), but the math is turning brutal: leave Azerbaijan 100+ up and the title is a formality. At 20, he'd smash Vettel's youngest-champion record by three full years. The one caveat: Baku punishes the slightest error, and he's never raced here with a title on the line.
  • Hamilton's Madrid zero flips the P2 fight — Hamilton left Spain with nothing while Russell took P5, turning a 10-point deficit into a 20-point one (211–191). Baku is a good place for a response: Hamilton won here in 2018 and Ferrari's low-drag package has been strong on power tracks. Norris (186) is now just 5 behind him — P2 through P5 are covered by 44 points with nine rounds left.
  • Verstappen's best shot at win #1 — P2 in Madrid was his strongest Sunday of 2026, and now he arrives at a circuit where he's won twice, including 2025's first-ever pole-to-flag run. Still winless this season, sixth in the standings — but Red Bull's straight-line efficiency and Baku's 2.2 km blast are the best matchup he's had all year. If it doesn't happen here, it may not happen at all.
  • Lawson keeps delivering in the senior seat — P6 in Madrid while deputizing for the injured Hadjar, who he now trails by just 12 points despite far fewer races in the big car. Every weekend Hadjar's wrist keeps him out, the 2027 seat conversation gets louder. Tsunoda continues filling in at Racing Bulls alongside rookie Lindblad.
  • Norris converts pole into... P3 — Madrid was the season in miniature: McLaren quick on Saturday, passed on Sunday. Antonelli beat him off the line, Verstappen undercut him. McLaren (306) has slipped 52 behind Ferrari in the constructors and now has Red Bull (230) closing from behind. Baku's slipstream lottery cuts both ways for a car that qualifies well.
  • Saturday race, morning start — The Thursday–Saturday format means lights out at 08:00 BRT Saturday, with qualifying Friday at 09:00 BRT. Set the alarm accordingly; historically ~70% of Baku races feature a safety car, so don't wander off mid-race.
