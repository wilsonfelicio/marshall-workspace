Built in 2004 for MotoGP, which shows: fast, flowing, almost no slow corners — the closest thing on the calendar to a permanent Silverstone-style rhythm track in the desert. The lap is dominated by medium- and high-speed corners, capped by the 1.07 km main straight into T1, the only true braking-zone pass. The T12–13–14 triple-apex right is the signature section, Maggotts-Becketts energy through the front-left tyre — which is exactly why Pirelli keeps imposing stint limits here. Night race; sand off-line cuts grip and breeds safety cars. Lap record 1:22.384 (Norris, 2024).

F1 edition in 2026
A bike track that bites. Lusail opened in 2004 as Qatar's MotoGP home and joined F1 in 2021 as a COVID-calendar stand-in — then signed a 10-year deal from 2023, complete with rebuilt pits and paddock. The flowing motorcycle DNA never left: it's one of the highest lateral-energy tracks of the year, and the pyramid kerbs plus relentless fast corners have forced Pirelli into intervention three times — an emergency 18-lap stint cap in 2023 after sidewall damage was found, and mandatory maximum-stint rules in 2025 and again this year. The two-stop is compulsory in 2026.
Winners' roll: Hamilton ('21), then Verstappen, Verstappen, Verstappen ('23–'25). Max has never lost an F1 Grand Prix at Lusail and clinched his third world title here in the 2023 Saturday sprint. Mercedes arrives having already sealed both 2026 championships — and Antonelli has unfinished business after last year's controversial ending (see below).
Where to pass: T1 after the kilometre-long main straight is the banker; the inside line into the tight T6 left is the lunge spot; the braking zone into the T10 left offers a third look. Beyond that it's about tyre delta — with two forced stops, offset strategies and big pace differences late in stints do the overtaking. Sand drifting onto the racing line punishes anyone forced off-line, and safety cars are common.
Iconic Lusail Moments
  • 🏆 2021 — Alonso's 105-race wait ends: Hamilton dominated the inaugural race in the middle of his epic title fight with Verstappen, but the night belonged to Fernando Alonso — P3 for Alpine, his first podium in seven years. Late-race punctures for Bottas, Russell and Latifi showed early what Lusail does to tyres.
  • 🥵 2023 — The brutality race: Verstappen clinched his third world title in the Saturday sprint (Piastri's first F1 win), then Sunday became a survival test: 36°C heat and 80% humidity under the lights left Sargeant retiring ill, Ocon vomiting in his helmet, and several drivers needing medical checks. Pirelli's emergency 18-lap stint cap after kerb-induced sidewall damage turned it into a flat-out triple sprint.
  • 🪞 2024 — Mirror, debris, chaos: Alonso's wing mirror fell off and sat on the main straight; the race turned on it. Norris got a stop-go for not lifting under the yellow, Hamilton and Sainz picked up punctures from the shattered glass, and Verstappen — demoted one grid slot for impeding Russell in quali, triggering a public feud — won anyway and set about Russell afterwards: "I lost all respect."
  • 🎲 2025 — McLaren's safety-car blunder: An early safety car and the mandatory-stint rules handed McLaren a free stop — the pit wall left both cars out. Verstappen pitted, inherited track position, and won; the title fight he should have been eliminated from went to Abu Dhabi. Widely rated the costliest strategy call of the season.
  • ⚔️ 2025 — The Antonelli affair: In the closing laps, Antonelli slipped wide and Norris went through for P4 — and Red Bull's Helmut Marko and race engineer Gianpiero Lambiase publicly suggested the Mercedes rookie had let the McLaren past deliberately. The accusation (later walked back with an apology) poisoned the run-in. One year on, Antonelli returns as world champion.

📰 Key Stories Heading Into Qatar

  • Verstappen owns this place — and P2 is nearly his — Max survived a late Mercedes onslaught to win in Vegas (his fourth win of 2026) and sits on 304, 37 clear of Hamilton for runner-up with two rounds left. He's won every Grand Prix ever held at Lusail in the ground-effect era — three in a row — and clinched a title here in 2023. Meanwhile Helmut Marko is already stirring 2027: "Verstappen will challenge Antonelli next year."
  • Three Brits, three points, one podium position — The real championship fight left: Hamilton 267, Russell 264, Norris 264 for P3 in the standings. Hamilton jumped ahead in Vegas while Russell clouted the wall trying to pass Antonelli and limped home P9. Leclerc's Vegas unraveled with a penalty for colliding with his own 2027 question-mark teammate Hamilton — one point scored, P6 overall at 250.
  • Red Bull vs McLaren: nine points for P3 and tens of millions — Red Bull 440, McLaren 431 after Vegas cut the gap. Andrea Stella reckons Lusail's fast, flowing corners suit the McLaren far better than the Strip did — and this is the track where his pit wall threw away last year's race. Prize-money stakes, revenge narrative, two rounds left.
  • The track Pirelli's tyres can't cope with — For the second straight year (third intervention since 2023), stint limits force a mandatory two-stop race. Lusail's fast corners load the front-left harder than anywhere else on the calendar; strategy converges, so grid position and the timing of the two windows decide everything. With the 2026 cars running Overtake Mode instead of DRS, nobody is certain how potent the kilometre-long main straight tow will be.
  • Antonelli's redemption lap — The 20-year-old returns to the scene of last year's "did he let Norris past?" storm as the youngest world champion in F1 history — 402 points, nine wins, both titles sealed with races to spare. Mercedes calls the Vegas defeat "the best reason to go racing again"; a Lusail win would be a pointed answer to the Red Bull camp that accused him a year ago.
