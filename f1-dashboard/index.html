Brand new and genuinely different: a hybrid street/permanent circuit wrapped around the IFEMA Exhibition Centre near Barajas, designed by Studio Dromo (Zandvoort's banking, Spa's renovation). The signature is La Monumental — a ~550 m semicircular banked corner at 24% gradient (13.5°), the longest banked turn in F1, ringed by a 45,000-seat grandstand; cars are in it for around six seconds. Add two tunnel sections under a motorway, real elevation change, a fast Valdebebas esses sequence, a slow technical "Bunker" section, and two straight-mode (overtake-boost) zones. Projected lap ~1:34.4. Capacity 110,000 — sold out.

No lap record exists. Whoever sets pole on Saturday writes the first line of this circuit's record book; Sunday's fastest lap becomes the race lap record. Projected pace is around 1:34.4. First-ever winner at a brand-new circuit is a distinction that lasts forever — think Hamilton at Sochi, Verstappen at Jeddah, Russell at Vegas.
Madrid's F1 past: the capital last hosted F1 at Jarama, 30 km north, in 1981 — 45 years ago. Madring makes Madrid the seventh Spanish venue to hold a championship round, after Pedralbes, Jarama, Montjuïc, Jerez, Valencia and Barcelona-Catalunya, which held the Spanish GP continuously from 1991 through 2025.
Where to pass: the designers promise "long straights and big stops". Prime zones: the main straight (straight-mode zone) into the heavy T1 braking; the second straight-mode zone between T3 and T4; and the exit of La Monumental slinging cars onto the following straight. On a narrow street-style surface off-line grip will be poor early in the weekend — expect divebombs, and expect the stewards to be busy.
Iconic Spanish GP Moments
  • 🛡️ 1981 — Villeneuve's masterclass (Jarama): Gilles Villeneuve parked his evil-handling Ferrari on the racing line for 67 laps and won with four cars swarming his gearbox — top five covered by 1.24s. Madrid's last F1 race was an all-timer.
  • ⚔️ 1986 — 0.014 seconds (Jerez): Senna and Mansell drag-raced to the line after Mansell's late tyre stop; the Lotus won by 93 centimetres — still one of the closest finishes ever.
  • 🔥 1991 — sparks on the straight (Barcelona): Mansell and Senna wheel-to-wheel down the front straight, inches apart at 300 km/h, sparks flying — the defining image of early-90s F1.
  • 🌧️ 1996 — the Regenmeister arrives: Michael Schumacher's first Ferrari win, lapping 3–4 seconds faster than anyone in a monsoon at Barcelona.
  • 🤯 2012 — Maldonado, seriously: Pastor Maldonado held off Alonso's Ferrari for Williams' last win before 2025 — the biggest shock of the decade.
  • 👶 2016 — a teenager wins on debut: Max Verstappen, 18, won his first race for Red Bull after the Mercedes wiped each other out on lap 1 — youngest winner in F1 history, a record that may never fall.
  • 🏠 2013 — Alonso's home glory: Fernando Alonso's last home win, celebrated with a Spanish flag handed to him on the cool-down lap. He's on the grid this weekend, 45 years after Madrid last saw F1.

📰 Key Stories Heading Into Madrid

  • A brand-new track, and nobody has a notebook — F1 races in Madrid for the first time since Jarama 1981, on a circuit that only exists in simulators. Hybrid street/permanent layout around the IFEMA halls: 22 corners, two tunnels, elevation, a slow "Bunker" complex and the 550 m, 24%-banked La Monumental. Green track, unknown bumps, walls close by — Friday running will be worth more than at any race this season, and the team that decodes setup first can bank a result the car doesn't deserve.
  • Antonelli's march — 267 points and counting — Seven wins in 13 races, a 66-point lead over his own teammate, and he arrives fresh off winning his home race at Monza despite the engine-penalty scare. At 20, he's closing on the youngest championship in history (Vettel won his at 23). The math: if he leaves Madrid 75+ clear of Russell, Mercedes can start planning the party. A new circuit is the great equalizer — and the only realistic way this title race tightens.
  • Sainz gets a true home race at last — Carlos Sainz was born in Madrid; this race is literally in his city, and the crowd will be his. The awkward part: he's P16 with 6 points in a Williams that has scored 11 all year. Alonso's return to a Madrid F1 race 45 years after Jarama is the other half of the Spanish story — in an Aston Martin that has 3 points and sits ahead of only Cadillac. Expect enormous grandstand energy and, on current form, not much home silverware.
  • Red Bull's revolving door, weekend three — Isack Hadjar's cracked wrist keeps him out again: Liam Lawson stays in the senior Red Bull seat (P7 at Zandvoort, points at Monza) and Tsunoda fills in at Racing Bulls. Meanwhile Verstappen took P3 at Monza but is still winless in 2026 — sixth in the championship, 140 points off the lead, in the strangest season of his career.
  • Russell vs Hamilton, round two of the P2 fight — Russell leads it 201 to 191 after finishing second at Monza. Hamilton, in Ferrari red with a home-crowd win at Barcelona-Catalunya already this season, has been the most consistent podium scorer since summer. Ferrari (346) still trails Mercedes (468) by 122 in the constructors — realistically defending P2 from McLaren (287) rather than attacking P1.
  • Does the overtaking promise hold? — Madring was pitched explicitly as the anti-Barcelona: big stops, two straight-mode zones, a banked corner that should allow side-by-side running. The 2026 active-aero cars with override boost are unproven on a layout like this. If it works, F1 gets a new classic; if it doesn't, expect a very loud told-you-so from the Montmeló faithful whose race got demoted to a rotation slot.
