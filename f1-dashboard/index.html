⚠️ Standard weekend — no Sprint, unlike Silverstone. Europe's heatwave breaks right as the paddock arrives: Friday practice carries a real thunderstorm threat (31% rain), but the current read is a dry Saturday qualifying (~0%) and a cool, mostly-dry race (~16°C, 10–28% rain, showers holding off until after the flag). That's Spa's trap — the Ardennes microclimate can leave one half of the 7 km lap soaking and the other bone dry, so the forecast is a suggestion, not a promise. The overtaking is real here: DRS down the Kemmel Straight into Les Combes is the single best passing spot on the calendar, so grid position matters less than at most tracks.

The longest lap in Formula 1 and widely called the greatest track in the world. Carved through the Ardennes forest, it climbs and plunges over 100 m of elevation. The signature is Eau Rouge-Raidillon (T3-4): a compression at the bottom, then a blind, flat-out uphill left-right taken near 300 km/h — nerve, not skill, is the limiter. It feeds the long Kemmel Straight, home to the calendar's best overtake into Les Combes. Add Pouhon, the flat-out Blanchimont, and the Bus Stop chicane, and it's the ultimate driver's test.

Most wins (driver): Schumacher (6, record) · Senna (5) · then a cluster incl. Hamilton (4) and Verstappen (3). Spa launched Schumacher's F1 career — his debut here in 1991 and first win here in 1992.
Most constructor wins: Ferrari leads the all-time Spa win count, with McLaren close behind — the honour roll spans nearly every great team in the sport's history.
Lap record: 1:44.701 — Sergio Pérez (Red Bull RB20, 2024). Piastri's 1:40.510 in 2025 Sprint qualifying is the outright track record but not an official race lap.
The real overtake spots: Les Combes (T5) at the end of the Kemmel Straight DRS zone is the best passing place in F1. Add the La Source hairpin off the line and the Bus Stop chicane. Eau Rouge is a flat-out commitment corner, not a passing place — the spectacle there is the speed.
Iconic Moments
  • 🌟 1992 — a legend is born: Michael Schumacher took the first of his record 91 wins here in only his first full season, reading a drying track to perfection. Spa remained his spiritual home for the rest of his career.
  • 🌧️ 1998 — the 13-car pile-up: A monsoon start triggered chaos at La Source that wiped out a third of the grid on lap one. The restarted race ended with Schumacher furiously confronting a lapped Coulthard after a crash in the spray.
  • ⚡ 2000 — the greatest overtake: Häkkinen used a backmarker as a pick to pass Schumacher on both sides into Les Combes at 320 km/h — routinely voted the best move in F1 history.
  • 🚀 2019 — Leclerc's first, amid tragedy: Charles Leclerc scored his maiden F1 win for Ferrari the day after Anthoine Hubert died in an F2 crash at Raidillon — an emotional victory he dedicated to his friend.
  • 🏳️ 2021 — the race that never was: Torrential rain meant just two laps behind the safety car before it was abandoned. Verstappen was awarded half points as "winner" — the shortest race in F1 history and a catalyst for rule changes.
  • 💨 2022 — Verstappen from 14th: A grid penalty sent Max to P14; he carved through the field and led inside a dozen laps, winning by nearly half a minute in a display that defined Red Bull's dominant era.

📰 Key Stories Heading Into Spa

  • The vanishing cushion — Antonelli's lead is crumbling — It was 66 points over Hamilton at Barcelona. It's now 32 over Hamilton and just 25 over Russell. The rookie has managed one podium and a Sprint win in the last three weekends while mechanical gremlins bit in both Barcelona and Silverstone — a wheel-shield failure cost him a shot at the British GP win and dropped him out of the points. He still leads, but the momentum has flipped, and Spa is where the chasers smell blood.
  • Hamilton, one win from history — in Ferrari red — Lewis sits P3 on 147, seven behind Russell, and arrives at Spa one victory away from equalling Schumacher's record six Belgian GP wins. A four-time winner here and one of the fiercest wet-weather drivers on the grid, he'd take that record in scarlet. If Friday's storms bleed into the weekend, he's the most dangerous man in the Ardennes.
  • Russell's shot at a straight fight — George is historically strong at Spa and now within 25 of his teammate. Silverstone was messy — he salvaged second while Antonelli's day fell apart — but a clean, dry qualifying (as forecast) plays to his single-lap strength. This is the chance to take the title fight to Antonelli head-on, something he hasn't managed since Canada.
  • Ferrari have found form — Leclerc owns this place — The Scuderia have won two of the last three races, Leclerc taking Silverstone for 29 points to climb to P4 on 108. Spa is where Charles scored his maiden F1 win in 2019, and the Ferrari-heavy Ardennes crowd will be loud. With both cars flying, Ferrari are the clearest threat to the Mercedes stranglehold.
  • Mercedes still the team to beat — A drivers' 1-2 and a 78-point constructors' cushion (333 to Ferrari's 255). Spa rewards top speed, efficiency and confidence through the high-speed sections — exactly the W17's strengths. Barring a weather lottery, the win should stay silver; the only real question is which Arrow takes it.
  • Silly season heats up — Verstappen and Piastri in the rumour mill — Driver-market whispers are swirling around Max and Oscar as the paddock reaches Belgium. Verstappen (P7, 76) is a three-time Spa winner and a wet-weather master who won here from 14th in 2022; Piastri (P6, 82) set the outright track record in 2025 Sprint qualifying (1:40.510). Both are underperforming their talent in 2026 — and both have futures suddenly worth talking about.
  • The weather is the co-favourite — The heatwave breaks on arrival: Friday practice carries a genuine thunderstorm threat before a drier Saturday and a cool race day. But Spa's lap is long enough to rain at Les Combes while the pit straight stays dry — every red-flagged, grid-scrambling classic here started with clouds over the Ardennes. One wet session and the title math blows wide open.
  • No Sprint, all track — After Silverstone's frantic Sprint, this is a traditional weekend: three practices to dial in a car for the sport's most demanding lap, then one qualifying shot and 44 laps on Sunday. Get the setup compromise wrong between the fast middle sector and the long straights and there's nowhere to hide over 7 km.
