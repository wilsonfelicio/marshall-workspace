Back to a normal weekend format after the Zandvoort Sprint — three full practice sessions to dial in the skinniest wings of the year. Late-summer Lombardy is usually warm and sunny, but forecasts this far out are indicative only; afternoon thunderstorms rolling off the Alps are always a possibility in September. The Temple of Speed. Built in 1922 in a royal park north of Milan, Monza is the oldest circuit on the calendar and has hosted the Italian GP every F1 season since 1950 bar one (1980). It's the fastest lap of the year: ~80% full throttle, four long straights interrupted by two heavy-braking chicanes, the twin Lesmo corners, the Ascari complex, and the endless Parabolica that slings cars back onto the 1.1 km main straight. Teams run their thinnest wings of the season, tow games dominate qualifying, and slipstream battles make it one of the most passable tracks in F1. The heaviest braking zone — ~340 to ~85 km/h into the Rettifilo chicane — is the prime overtaking spot.

Most wins (driver): Michael Schumacher and Lewis Hamilton share the Italian GP record with 5 victories apiece. Hamilton's first came in 2012 for McLaren; Schumacher's five all came in red, which is why the tifosi still sing his name.
Most constructor wins: Ferrari, with 20 home victories — no team has won any race more often at a single circuit. Every one triggers the famous podium invasion, with the crowd flooding the main straight under the rostrum.
Lap record: 1:21.046 — Rubens Barrichello (Ferrari, 2004), still standing from the V10 era. The fastest lap in F1 history was also set here: Hamilton's 2020 pole at an average of 264.362 km/h. With 2026's low-drag active-aero cars, both marks are live targets this weekend.
Where to pass: the ~340→85 km/h braking zone into the Rettifilo chicane (Turn 1) at the end of the 1.1 km main straight is the biggest stop of the season and the prime move. The Roggia chicane after the Curva Grande tow is option two, and late lunges into Ascari happen every year. Slipstream is worth ~0.3s a lap here — expect trains, tows and DRS-era-style drafting games even under the new override rules.
Iconic Moments
  • ⚡ 1971 — the closest finish ever: Peter Gethin won by 0.01s over Ronnie Peterson in a five-car slipstream drag to the line — the top five covered by 0.61 seconds. Pre-chicane Monza at its absurd best.
  • 🖤 1978 — Peterson's last race: a startline pile-up claimed Ronnie Peterson's life and reshaped F1 safety. Mario Andretti clinched the title that day in the saddest way possible.
  • ❤️ 1988 — for Enzo: weeks after Enzo Ferrari's death, Gerhard Berger and Michele Alboreto delivered a Ferrari 1–2 — the only race all season McLaren's dominant MP4/4 didn't win. Monza does script-writing.
  • 🌧️ 2008 — a star is born: 21-year-old Sebastian Vettel took pole and won in the rain in a Toro Rosso, becoming F1's then-youngest winner in the team's only victory.
  • 🔴 2019 — Leclerc holds the line: Charles Leclerc fended off both Mercedes for the tifosi's first home win since 2010, a week after his first career victory at Spa.
  • 🤯 2020 — Gasly's miracle: a red flag, a penalty for Hamilton, and Pierre Gasly held off Sainz for AlphaTauri's shock win — one of the great modern upsets.
  • 💥 2021 — the halo earns its keep: Verstappen and Hamilton collided at the Rettifilo, Max's car landing on Lewis's halo. Daniel Ricciardo led a McLaren 1–2 through the chaos.

📰 Key Stories Heading Into Italy

  • Antonelli's home race — from the back of the grid — The script wrote itself: the 20-year-old Italian championship leader, first home GP as title favourite, tifosi split between him and Ferrari. Then Mercedes confirmed a complete new power unit beyond his season allocation, sending him to the back of the grid. The logic is cold championship math — the old PU cost him DNFs in Barcelona and Silverstone, and Monza's slipstream-friendly layout is the cheapest place on the calendar to serve the penalty. He leads by 59 points (242 vs 183); a recovery drive into the top five would barely dent the cushion, but a first-lap chicane melee is the nightmare scenario.
  • Norris is the form man — and the only live threat — Back-to-back wins in Hungary and Zandvoort have dragged Lando to P4, 83 points behind with 10 rounds to go after Monza. Stella says the upgraded MCL40 lets him drive "at a World Champion level," and with Antonelli starting last, Monza is McLaren's best chance yet to make this a championship again. He won here from the front in the past; a third straight win would change the season's temperature completely.
  • Ferrari brings an engine upgrade to the temple — The Scuderia arrives at home P2 in both championships, with Hamilton tied on 183 with Russell and racing at Monza in red for the first time. An 067/6 power unit upgrade is expected this weekend, aimed squarely at Mercedes. Hamilton has 5 Italian GP wins, Leclerc has won here twice (2019, 2024) — and with the Mercedes of Antonelli buried in the pack at the start, the tifosi will smell blood.
  • Hadjar targets a comeback, Red Bull juggles seats — Isack Hadjar (P8, 68 pts) aims to return from the wrist injury — a "small crack" in his left hand — that ruled him out of Zandvoort. Liam Lawson deputised well at Red Bull (P7 in the Dutch GP) and Tsunoda filled the Racing Bulls seat; if Hadjar isn't cleared, that shuffle repeats. Meanwhile Verstappen crashed out of his home race on lap 1 and is still winless in 2026 — P6 and 130 points off the lead, at a track where Red Bull's low-drag efficiency used to be a weapon.
  • Russell vs Hamilton, tied at 183 — The old Mercedes teammates are dead level for P2, split only on countback. Russell has out-scored everyone but Antonelli since Canada but keeps losing Sundays to reliability and penalties; Hamilton has quietly been the most consistent podium collector since summer. Whoever wins this duel likely finishes best-of-the-rest — and at Monza they're in different cars for the first time.
  • New-era cars at the fastest track — This is the first time the 2026 active-aero, override-boost cars race at Monza's low-drag extreme. Straight-line speeds could nudge 360 km/h, Barrichello's 22-year-old lap record is genuinely under threat, and nobody quite knows how the energy-recovery-heavy power units will handle four long full-throttle blasts per lap. Expect engineers sweating deployment maps as much as drivers sweating the chicanes.
