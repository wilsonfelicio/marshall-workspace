A street circuit threading the streets of Monte Carlo since 1929 — barriers everywhere, zero run-off, and the slowest corner in F1 at the Fairmont Hairpin (~48 km/h). The Tunnel is the only flat-out section; the Nouvelle Chicane after it is the single real passing spot. The shortest lap on the calendar, and the one where a tenth in qualifying is worth a place on Sunday. Get it wrong and you're in the wall.

Iconic Moments
  • 🌀 1988 — Senna's lap from another dimension: Senna qualified 1.4s clear of teammate Prost in identical machinery, later describing being "in a tunnel… beyond conscious understanding." Still cited as the greatest qualifying lap in F1 history. He then crashed out of a huge lead on Sunday — Monaco giveth and taketh.
  • 🛡️ 1992 — Senna holds off Mansell: On worn tyres in a slower McLaren, Senna defended every inch from a charging Nigel Mansell over the final laps. Mansell threw everything at him; Senna never cracked. A masterclass in the art of the impossible-to-pass defence.
  • 🌧️ 1997 — Schumacher's wet masterclass: In a flooded Monte Carlo, Schumacher won by 53 seconds for Ferrari — the Scuderia's first Monaco win since 1981. He simply drove on a different planet to the rest of the field.
  • 👑 King of Monaco: Senna won five in a row (1989–1993) and six total — the record. Graham Hill ("Mr. Monaco") and Schumacher sit on five each. Win here and you join an exclusive club.
  • 🏠 2024 — Leclerc breaks the home curse: After years of Monaco heartbreak, the Monégasque finally won his home race for Ferrari — emotional scenes on the streets where he grew up. He arrives in 2026 desperate to do it again.
  • 🏛️ The setting: Yachts in the harbour, the casino, the Tunnel, Tabac, the Swimming Pool section. Glamour on the surface, brutal precision underneath. There is no margin — the walls are right there, all lap, every lap.

📰 Key Stories Heading Into Monaco

  • Antonelli on four straight — but Monaco is the great equalizer — The 19-year-old leads by 43 points after winning a fourth consecutive race in Canada. The form driver of the era arrives at the one circuit where the car matters less and the single qualifying lap matters most. Monaco has humbled prodigies before. Can he keep his nerve where a tenth on Saturday is worth a podium on Sunday?
  • Russell's "somebody doesn't want me to fight" — PU failure in Canada — Russell was leading the charge against his teammate when a power-unit failure ended his Canadian GP. "It feels like somebody doesn't want me to fight for this championship," he said in disbelief. He's now 43 back. Intra-Mercedes tension is the quiet subplot — the team is running away with the constructors (219 pts) but the title fight is increasingly one-sided.
  • Leclerc's home race — can he repeat 2024? — The Monégasque ended his infamous home curse with a maiden Monaco win in 2024. He sits P3 in the standings, 13 behind Russell, and there is no race he wants more. Ferrari are a clear P2 in constructors. On the streets he grew up on, a perfect lap on Saturday is the whole ballgame.
  • Verstappen — the street-circuit specialist in a struggling car — Max is a two-time Monaco winner and arguably the best one-lap driver on the grid. Red Bull are a dismal P4 (57 pts) and Max sits P7, but Monaco's low-speed, point-and-squirt nature can mask aero deficiencies. He took P3 in Canada after a race-long scrap with Hamilton. Don't discount him here — Saturday is his weapon.
  • Qualifying is the race — Overtaking at Monaco is near-impossible, and the wider, heavier 2026 cars make it harder still. With a single DRS zone and the Nouvelle Chicane as the only real passing spot, track position is everything. Expect strategy games, undercuts, and a Saturday afternoon that effectively decides the podium.
  • McLaren need to bounce back — Norris took Monaco pole in 2025 and holds the qualifying lap record (1:09.954), so the package can sing here. But both McLarens retired in Canada — a costly weekend that let Mercedes and Ferrari pull clear. P3 in constructors, they need points and a clean weekend on a track that historically suits them.
  • First Monaco for the 2026 regulations — The new lighter, active-aero cars hit the tightest circuit on the calendar for the first time. Unknowns abound around the Fairmont Hairpin and the chicanes, where the new aero behaves differently at crawling speed. Setup and confidence in the walls will separate the field.
  • Calendar shuffle — Monaco moves to June — For 2026, Monaco and Canada were swapped for logistics and sustainability, putting the crown jewel in early June rather than its traditional late-May slot. Same glamour, same walls, same impossible-to-pass reality — just a few weeks later.
