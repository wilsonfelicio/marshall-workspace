A lap of Sepang: launch down the ~900 m pit straight to T1, an entry so wide that three cars can arrive side by side — the prime passing zone, with around-the-outside a real option. T2 spits you toward the uphill run to T4, stop number three on the overtake list. Then the track starts flowing: the fast T5–T6 esses, the long double-apex T7–T8 rights that quietly murder front tyres in 32°C heat, and the second-gear T9 hairpin. The middle sector builds speed through T10–T13 before everything funnels into T14: nail the exit, because what follows is the back straight, the boost zone, and the T15 hairpin — miss your braking there and your rival gets the tow all the way down the pit straight for another go at T1. Two chances to pass per lap, every lap, and an equatorial thunderstorm on standby: Tilke never topped his first attempt.
